## Immediate corrections and teaching distinctions

1. **Dhōd worksheet has no target in the printed passage.** The screenshot asks for ض in Al-Fatihah ayat 1–5; the two occurrences are in ayah 7. Select a passage containing the target, or explicitly make “none here” a supported activity. The live Inspire screen correctly includes the whole surah and reports two matching words; the inconsistency appears in the screenshot of the printed journey sheet. Calculate both letter occurrences and matching words from the same selected passage; do not conflate the two counts. Source: [Al-Fatihah](https://quran.com/al-fatihah).
2. **The jawf entry must include all three madd letters.** The full Sound Spots buttons show ا و ي, while the summary card/print chart appears to show only Alif. Explain the madd conditions: alif after fathah, silent waw after dammah, silent ya after kasrah. Consonantal waw/ya belong to other articulation groups. One isolated Alif is not a full representation of al-jawf.
3. **Closed lips are not the consonantal Waw posture.** The existing “Both lips” drawing is closed while listing Ba, Mim and Waw. Keep the shared group but give Waw a rounded opening. Ba and Mim also differ in release/nasal sound; a common static closure alone does not explain either sound completely.
4. **Lam needs tip AND adjoining front edges.** “Front edges” alone is incomplete. The new sample highlights the front arc. The teal arrows are schematic lateral airflow cues, not measured airflow. Asatizah should approve both their direction and the visual simplification.
5. **Dhōd is not a tongue-tip contact.** Use one or both lateral tongue edges against the upper molar region. Keep top view as the primary explanation; the sagittal dot only locates the broad region. A midsagittal picture cannot directly show lateral contact. Left/right names in exports refer to the illustration; avoid implying a preferred learner side.
6. **Do not teach Ra as obligatory repeated rolling.** A gentle articulation cue is useful, but distinguish a single controlled contact from deliberate repeated trilling. Your brief’s “one tap” should remain a simplification checked by a teacher, rather than a pronunciation diagnosis.
7. **Ghunnah is a resonance route, not a different oral home for Nun/Mim.** Show the nasal route in addition to the tongue/lip closure. Do not tell children to press or pinch hard or put fingers inside the mouth. Let adult guidance determine whether the nose demonstration is suitable.
8. **Heavy-letter colour must not replace articulation.** Do not move all seven heavy letters to one back-of-tongue dot. Qof, Kho/Ghain and the emphatic letters retain different primary contacts; the additional raised-back posture is a separate feature. Detailed cutaway remains for the next review round.

Suggested teacher cross-check: **Kareema Carol Czerepinski, Tajweed Rules of the Qur’an, Part One**, articulation section, [PDF hosted by IslamHouse](https://d1.islamhouse.com/data/en/ih_books/single2/en_Tajweed_Rules_of_the_Quran_Part_01.pdf). Sample drawings remain schematics and are marked DRAFT on each image. This is a design/content review, not teacher sign-off.
